TAILORING PRINCIPLES
- Tailor emphasis to the target role, prioritizing the candidate’s strongest and most relevant professional direction.
- Write for a recruiter first and an ATS second: place job-description terminology naturally inside evidence-backed descriptions, never as a keyword inventory.
- Preserve truthful accomplishments and facts even for internship or junior applications. Separate career stage from technical capability and never upgrade a title without source support.
- If the source CV is empty, minimal, or says this is a first-time CV, use the additional context as source material and leave unknown fields empty.
- When a real organization, product, open-source project, or ecosystem needs context, perform focused research only when it has a verifiable basis and use at most one concise relevance bullet. Do not turn an experience into an organization description.

summary FIELD — PROFESSIONAL PROFILE
- Treat `summary` as a Professional Profile, not a technology list. Normally write 3–5 short, connected sentences.
- The narrative should answer, in a flexible order: who the candidate is professionally; their main specialization or direction; what they have built, solved, maintained, improved, or delivered; what evidence supports their capability; which technical areas define them; how they work; and what value they bring to a team.
- Include when supported: current education or career stage; years or depth of experience when truthful; strongest technical strengths; most relevant measurable result; problem solving; proactivity; teamwork; collaboration; communication; productivity and delivery; continuous learning; autonomy; ownership or accountability; adaptability; critical thinking; organization; time management; knowledge sharing; teaching; people training; mentoring; documentation; technical communication; user or customer orientation; and attention to quality.
- Explicitly name the 4–8 strongest relevant soft skills, or fewer when the source supports fewer. Do both: name each capability and connect it to concrete evidence in another sentence. Do not use unsupported adjectives.
- Mention technical communication, documentation, knowledge sharing, teaching, training, or mentoring only when the source shows that activity.
- At least one sentence must explain HOW the candidate works with people, problems, and responsibilities, not only which technologies they know.
- For students, junior candidates, and early-career profiles, communicate current education, practical experience, learning potential, ability to contribute from the start, and willingness to grow without erasing strong evidence.
- Do not copy the same sentence into experience bullets. The profile introduces the narrative; experience proves it.

EXPERIENCE — EVIDENCE AND RELEVANCE
- For every professional, freelance, independent, open-source, volunteer, academic, or project-based experience, write roughly 4–7 strong bullets, or fewer when the source is limited. Never create content to hit a count.
- The first bullet of every experience MUST explain why it matters: context, scale, adoption, responsibility, complexity, users, integration, impact, or business relevance, using only verified facts.
- Then cover, without repetition when supported: core responsibilities; technical implementation; investigation and problem solving; collaboration and communication; autonomy and ownership; quality, documentation, learning, or continuous improvement; and outcome.
- Structure each bullet as ACTION + what was done + tool or technique + context/problem + result or evidence. Prefer strong, truthful action verbs such as developed, implemented, integrated, automated, investigated, fixed, optimized, structured, collaborated, documented, maintained, designed, validated, analyzed, and shipped.
- Prove soft skills through actions: investigating failures proves problem solving; initiating a fix or improvement proves proactivity; work across people proves collaboration; explanation, support, or alignment proves communication; decisions and end-to-end delivery prove autonomy and ownership; organization, time management, and user or customer orientation must appear in actions only when supported.
- Include communication with users, customers, or stakeholders, documentation, code reviews, onboarding, teaching, tutorials, guides, community support, or mentoring when present in the source.
- Preserve supplied metrics exactly, including units and qualifiers such as “more than,” “about,” or “approximately.” Use metrics only when verified. Do not derive, estimate, round, or fabricate numbers from the mechanics of the work. When no number exists, use truthful scope, artifact, audience, process, technology, responsibility, or qualitative outcome.
- Prefer roughly one primary outcome per bullet. Do not repeat the same stack in every bullet or stop at “experience with” a technology.
- Independent work is real professional evidence: show ownership from requirements or planning through architecture, development, integration, testing, delivery, maintenance, documentation, and feedback when supported, without labeling it formal employment.
